A picture made from executable marks. A small studio that keeps its experiments visible, including the ones that do not work.

What makes the picture?

The studio analyzes a reference and compiles an ordered collection of strokes. Each path carries geometry, colour, width, pressure, opacity, and material information. The player follows that geometry, and Canvas and SVG share the material primitives. No completed stylized image is revealed behind the pointer.

Reference + canvas cropsVisual encoder + recurrent memoryRegion manager + material workerRecorded paths + playback

The experimental visual route uses local reference/current/residual crops, global context, and a recurrent memory. Its manager chooses a region; its worker selects among sixteen procedural material proposals. The artist palettes, stage plans, tools, and important parts of the action grammar are authored. Preserved procedural and learned engines, a heuristic strategy, and direct stroke optimization remain available.

What was actually trained?

ComponentVerified release evidence
Experimental visual policy9,712,482 inference parameters. No pretrained vision or painting weights.
Supervised warm start8,192 updates; synthetic tool exercises and teacher candidate examples.
Main hierarchical PPO4,952 attempted episodes; 633,856 worker decisions. All 19 validation checks failed to beat the warm start. Selected RL episode: 0.
Selected worker calibrationActual-Canvas teacher demonstrations, followed by 12 supervised epochs. Only worker layers changed. This is not additional RL.
Corpus2,000 museum sources: 1,998 CMA CC0 records and two Met public-domain records. Artist-specific coverage is uneven.

The calibrated policy improved the reconstruction proxy by 5.6% against warm on a separate 18-case, 1,024-mark comparison. Direct optimization still performed substantially better on that proxy. These results do not establish learned creativity, historical authenticity, or universal portrait likeness. The accepted Recommended routes were retained.

Digital materials, with clear limits

Tools have different contact footprints, taper, pressure responses, bristle or strand geometry, and layering. They are physically motivated digital approximations. Alpha deposition is not a complete pigment optics solver; paper lift deposits paper colour; palette-knife ridges are decorative. Wet paint interaction and physical calibration remain future work.

Resolution is not the same as detail

Native-coordinate 3840 × 2160 and 2160 × 3840 exports, resizing, and recorded-action replay were tested. A native thin-line reconstruction diagnostic failed despite correct export dimensions. High-budget experimental pencil cases also failed to complete. Neither genuine 4K realism nor perfect anatomy is claimed.

Interpretation and composition

Artist modes use researched, authored preferences. They are not complete learned models of Leonardo, Dürer, and Rembrandt. Reference-free original composition, reliable semantic anatomy, structured textiles, and independent style assessment remain unproved. The generated landscape and muse on the exhibition page are decorative brand illustrations; the gallery separately shows supplied tool exports whose engine metadata was not provided.

Local by design

Uploads are processed on the device. There is no backend, authentication, hosted painting service, browser-time training, or analytics. The standalone file bundles the studio, worker, models, and assets. Hosted versions download the studio weights on entry. Pigment edits change a study’s settings, not the network weights.

Research figures are drawn from the saved release report dated 12 October 2026. Full reproducibility scripts, training manifests, unsuccessful comparisons, and checkpoints remain in the source handoff.
